## 1. Overall evaluation

Parola has become technically impressive in six days (113 commits since 27 September): a 6,958-word / 1,185-verb dictionary with complete morphology, a conjugation engine that got 137 hard verbs right across every mood and tense, a deterministic evidence model that replays identically on every device, a 256-lesson course engine with facets and repair, a sentence workshop that is the best screen in the app, 39 deterministic checks and 20 browser suites gating every deploy, and an atomic offline install. The dictionary content is clean: 0 errors in 280 entries read closely.

As a *learning product* it is not yet one thing, and the weakest part is the one the owner asked about most, the lessons themselves. In order of importance:

1. **The beginner course is a grammar-form drill with a very small world.** After Foundations and all 60 A1 lessons a learner has met 491 distinct Italian word forms; the A1 exercises use 146 content words, and the names *Anna* and *Luca* appear more often than any noun. Days of the week, months, colours, weather, numbers above twenty, *suo/sua*, *fratello/sorella*, *ti piace*, professions, clothes, directions and health are never taught at A1–A2. Plural imperfetto, plural future and the conditional paradigm are typed at B1/B2 without ever being taught. Three lessons teach wrong or unidiomatic Italian as the model, and most of Foundations–B2 is visibly generated: 16 distinct prompts cover 369 A2 questions, and 40 % of B1/B2 "teach" steps are auto-generated echoes of the previous answer. The hand-written lessons added on 2 October (A1 units 13–14, A2 units 7 and 15, B1 unit 12) show what the course should look like everywhere.

2. **The mastery, review and reward mechanics are four systems, not one.** "Learned" is granted after six multiple-choice taps, after two to four matching taps on a course board, or after three workshop drills whose answers may have been shown first, and in every case the evidence model itself still says the word is only *practicing*. No word can ever reach *ready* or *remembered* because no word path asks for a typed answer. Review lists one row per skill (four rows per word), replays a whole six-screen lesson per row, and in a 30-day simulation of a learner who reviews every morning the overdue list grows without bound to 263 words. A single verb tense chapter asks 42 questions, 32 of them typed, before its recap. XP rewards recognition over production (a one-minute matching board earns 40 XP, a seven-minute grammar lesson 6). The level ribbon, "current level" setting and streak measure nothing pedagogical.

3. **The navigation is three products in one shell.** Home, Learn and Play give three different answers to "what should I do now", and Home ignores a paused course lesson. Three unrelated notions of level (the ribbon's `settings.level`, the course stage, the study scope) can read B1, Foundations and A1 at the same moment. The Learn tab holds two complete views of the same model, both duplicating Home and Play. Five lesson players have five different chromes, exits and end screens. The workshop sits below the fold in a reel; placement is a ghost link at the bottom of the course page. One real bug: the Learn hero shows *credere* and its button resumes *mangiare*.

4. **The workshop is excellent on the surface and lenient underneath.** Its 21 lessons are idiomatic (8 small issues in 168 activities), the player is polished and the free-entry experience is the model for the rest of the app. But the grading silently re-inflects wrong forms: typing *stanchi*, *stanca* or even the English *tired* in "Bene grazie, ma sono ___" is graded *Esatto* and rewritten to *sono stanco*, so agreement, the thing the lesson is about, is never tested; meaning is never checked (*Marco piace la pizza*, *Studio l'italiano non* are "Accepted"); and the taught compound forms (*ho preso*, *sono andato*) are rejected as "not in the dictionary". The fit scorer's note is misleading with the real model (the lesson's own bank word *libero* reads "odd here"), and the assistant can never run on the shipped content: it fires only when a conversation turn has two or more generic reactions, and no turn has. The model that downloaded fine on the phone is therefore inert.

5. **The code is organised in generations, with two scaling defects.** Five generations of lesson UI were layered without consolidating the previous one (five players, six multiple-choice renderers, five question generators, six session shapes). The evidence log is unbounded and every answer re-normalises and deep-copies all of it: 10 ms per answer at 250 events, 50 ms at 2,000, half a second at 6,000, which a daily learner reaches in two months, and the whole profile is uploaded on every sync. The first screen waits for the whole dictionary, every course pack and a 640 KB verb table that only verb lessons need: 8.4 MB before anything is painted, 11 s on slow 4G, and still 2.6 s when served from the offline cache. The default verb lesson is routed through two retired modules before the real player loads. Offline, update and persistence, by contrast, are genuinely robust. None of this is hard to fix, and the pure-engine discipline makes it tractable.

6. **The dictionary and reference are in good shape, with two structural faults.** The build's "lowest level wins" merge leaves everyday words only in their rare C2 sense (*macchia* = maquis, *mora* = payment default, *giostra* = joust), and the codex's accent rules can never fire because `fold()` strips accents before the test, so every noun in *-tà/-tù/-è* (178 of them) gets a wrong or missing plural explanation.

The recommendation is a sequence of four rounds (§7): a short fixes round that removes wrong Italian and the grading holes now; a progression round that makes learning, review and reward one system; a cohesion round that makes the app one product; and a content round that rewrites A1–A2 around a real syllabus. On-device AI gets a measured, constrained role once the deterministic layer is right.

## 3. What works and should be kept

- **The engines.** `conjugator.js`, `learning/model.js`, `course-v2-engine.js` and `sentence-lab.js` are pure, Node-importable, contract-documented and tested; the import graph has no cycles; the `learnCourse.js` + `course-v2-activities.js` + engine trio is the right shape for every player.
- **The verb evidence model** (unaided, first-attempt, spaced, varied production per person; assistance tracked and excluded; mistakes keep a target open) is better than most commercial apps. Verb targets reviewed a day later become *remembered* and move to 8- then 20-day intervals as designed.
- **The course pipeline** is pedagogically honest: words → board → teach → guided → independent → repair → recap, exact resume with drafts, placement that awards nothing, listening evidence separated from reading, 130 reviewed audio clips, two LibriVox readings.
- **The sentence workshop**: roles, free entry with suggestions and drills, reactive conversations, Say it yourself, completion with XP and links back to the pattern; sessions saved only after the first action.
- **The dictionary data and notes**, the codex, Words, Play and the source picker.
- **Deployment discipline**: content-stamped atomic precache, `check-shell`, `build-site` verification, deploys gated on every suite, revision-checked sync.
- **Candid copy** about what is and is not assessed, and on-device privacy.

## 4. Findings by area

Severity: Critical blocks or misleads learning daily; High is wrong or confusing for most learners; Medium is a real defect with a workaround; Low is polish. Every item below is reproduced in the area report named in the heading, with ids and line numbers.

### 4.1 Course content (`course-content.md`)

Critical
- **Lexical poverty.** 491 distinct word forms after Foundations + A1 (447 in A1 alone), 750 after A2; A1 exercises use 146 content words; *Anna* ×179 and *Luca* ×138 outnumber every noun; A1 teaches two adjectives (*piccolo*, *grande*). Never taught at A1–A2: numbers 21–99, days beyond *lunedì/martedì/sabato*, months, dates, seasons, colours, weather beyond *piove*, *suo/sua/nostro/vostro*, *fratello/sorella/marito/moglie*, *ti/le/gli piace*, professions, nationalities, clothes, directions, health, *fa/scorso/prossimo*, *tra/fra*, *dire/uscire/sapere/bere/dare*.
- **Teach-after-test.** `v2-a1-want-need` repair items demand *vuole/deve* (lesson teaches *voglio, vuoi, devo*); imperfetto is taught for *io* and *lui/lei* only, then B1 types *viaggiavamo*, *preparavamo*, *avevamo*; future is taught as *-erò/-erai/-erà*, then B1 types *prenderemo*, *mangeremo*, *partiremo*, *torneremo*; the conditional paradigm is never taught but typed at B2.
- **Templating.** 16 distinct prompts over 369 A2 questions ("Choose the expression that fits this situation." is 63 % of A2); 60/60 A1 lessons are "7 min" with the same 11–14-step shape; 87/218 B1 and 82/214 B2 teach steps are auto-generated "Notice why it fits" echoes; 44 B1 and 38 B2 reserve items use the target's explanation as both hint and explanation; 26 of 96 C1 and C2 teach steps are the identical "Transfer the distinction" card; 34 exercise contexts are shared verbatim across lessons; 194 repair titles read "Try use sono, sei and è again".

High
- **Wrong or unidiomatic Italian taught as the model.** `v2-a1-near-far` enforces *vicina a* and rejects the standard invariable *vicino a* (strict items s9, review-0, review-1); `v2-a1-a-in-articles` teaches *Il libro è nella casa*; `v2-a2-compare-activities` builds the whole lesson on *Leggo più che scrivo* / *Cammino più che corro*; the B1 *ce ne* lessons conflate dative *ce* (*ce ne servono due*, "we need two") with locative *ce ne sono*.
- Wrong explanations and glosses: `v2-a2-direct-singular.s10` explains *la pizza* with "Casa is feminine singular"; `v2-a1-potere-requests.s6` "Madam, may you come in?"; spoken answers with a stray space after elision ("all' una", "l' ho") because `speak` is built by string replacement.
- Nine pronunciation lessons have no audio and test letter-spotting ("Which two vowel letters occur in casa?" → "a and a").
- Duplicated lessons: *tu* commands taught in A1 11.4 and again in A2 12.1; *a* + article taught three times; B1 story-sequence lessons repeat A2 material.
- C1/C2: inputs and portfolio tasks are C-level (Pirandello, Collodi, 200–300-word dossiers), but every graded target is B1/B2 grammar relabelled; comprehension checks are English multiple-choice, several answerable without the Italian; both lessons of `v2-c1-u1` share one passage.

Medium
- Invented distractors ("hano", "scrivuto", "parliato", "Sono ci", "Mi chiamo?"); *veduto* marked wrong; passages too short for the level (A1/A2 median 12 words, B1 23, B2 24); 17 % of A1 hints contain the answer; facets are nominal (one per lesson equal to the lesson id, or `use`); 71 Foundations/A1 facets have only two independent variants, so a second mistake on the same facet ends the lesson for eight hours; minutes are a constant.

### 4.2 Pedagogy and the learning model (`pedagogy.md`)

Critical
- **Four credit rules, all recognition.** Word lesson: six multiple-choice screens. Course board: two to four matches, the last one forced. Workshop drill: three hits out of up to six attempts with the answer shown between. Games: never. In all cases the model's state stays *practicing* and the store's `learned` flag, which drives the tick, the learned list, the level rings, game sources and the workshop's banks, is set by the views.
- **Review cannot be worked through.** One row per skill; Home shows the skill count (898 for 263 words after 30 days); each row replays the whole lesson; recognition sessions score SM-2 quality 3, which shortens intervals and lowers ease, so a known word comes back every 2–6 days for six screens. Removing the 40-row cap does not help: clearing the queue every day costs about 120 minutes a day by day 19 and is still rising, because no word session can earn the quality-4 step that would lengthen its interval.

High
- Words can never reach *ready* or *remembered*: no word path asks for a typed answer; the retention rule (two unaided successes a day later) is unreachable for ~7,000 of 8,100 entries.
- Workshop drills credit after the answer was shown (a repeat hit counts).
- XP is incoherent: board 40 XP/min, grammar lesson 1 XP/min; guided choice earns the same 2 XP as an unaided typed recall.
- No cross-module plan: course, verb lessons, word lessons, workshop, games and review each keep their own queue; nothing throttles new items against overdue ones.
- Two SRS systems coexist (`store.items` per entry for games and `markLearned`; `model.js` per skill for lessons) and games record under a different objective-id space, so game answers never advance a lesson's skill.

Medium
- A verb lesson's length depends on the verb's *spec*, not its difficulty (*fare* = *parlare*; *essere* is shorter); 164 questions and about an hour for one verb's five tenses plus the mix.
- Level rings are learned ÷ dictionary entries per CEFR level; "Current level" only colours the app; the streak counts days the app was opened (it reads "1 day" at first open with 0 XP).
- Verb review is per target, so one chapter yields up to 16 separate review sessions the next day.

### 4.3 Learner experience and navigation (`learner-experience.md`)

Critical
- Three answers to "what now": Home Continue → a verb lesson; Learn hero → the grammar lesson; Play → a quiz; with a course lesson paused at 35 % Home still says "learn 2 new verbs".
- Review as a screen: 30 due words = 30 rows and 30 six-screen lessons; verbs without chapter evidence never appear; changing the study scope to A2 hides all 30.
- A tense chapter asks 42 questions before its recap; the verb is learned only after five such chapters; Home asks for two new verbs a day.

High
- Learn hero shows *credere* and its "Resume lesson" button opens *mangiare* (`learnDash.js:139-142`).
- Opening a lesson creates a 0 % "In progress" thread; visiting the session page saves a session and flips the hero to "Resume session" (the workshop does this right).
- Three levels at once (ribbon, course stage, scope).
- Toasts cover the Continue button in every full-screen player (`app.css:230`), exactly when "1 parola imparata" fires on the boards.
- Learn is two full views (Panoramica/Sezioni) of one model, duplicating Home and Play; the workshop is below the fold in a reel at y≈1109 on a 932 px screen; placement is a ghost link; Review is the fourth card of a reel.
- Five chromes: course and workshop have a progress bar, the verb journey has pills and no bar; ⓘ means options in one and a chapter menu in the other; the title flickers "mangiare · lesson"/"Verb lesson"; course and word completions have no XP, stamp or confetti; games have an X, lessons need Pause → Back.
- Numbers disagree: Home "30 to review" vs Me "0 reviewing · 38 learning"; "Verbi 8 / 2 today"; a new "Learner" has a 1-day streak before doing anything; there is no onboarding.

Medium
- No naming rule: kicker and title language flips per screen (English → Italian on Home/Learn/Me, Italian → English on Words/Codex, English only on Course); "Practice/Practise" both; straight and curly apostrophes.
- Two dictionary indexes (Words, Reference) and two detail pages (entry, codex); Recently viewed only on Reference.
- The "Insieme" session picks its verb alphabetically (*abbracciare*).
- Course page, Review and placement are older-generation screens; Me is a 10,000 px settings wall with the sync form inline.
- Due reviews depend on the study scope.

### 4.4 Sentence workshop, useful words and on-device AI (`workshop-and-ai.md`)

Content (High, small): *dormo sempre tardi* glossed "go to sleep late"; the reaction *Stanco?* shown to a learner who wrote *sono stanca*; *resteremo a casa e guardiamo un film*; *ha quaranta anni*; the *piacere* model card tags *mi* as the subject; six reactions end in a question the fixed next line ignores.

Grading (Critical)
- Silent re-inflection graded correct: *stanchi*, *stanca*, *tired*, *sono tired* → "sono stanco · correct"; *mangiato*, *mangiare*, *eat* → *mangia*; the bank trap *sono stanchi* in `sl-strutture-06` is graded correct in the browser.
- Meaning-blind: *Marco piace la pizza*, *Marco dorme la pizza*, *Studio l'italiano non*, *più migliore* are "Accepted · your own words"; Say it yourself keeps *Io sono una studentessa* for a male speaker and *Mia sorella è un fratello* for 15 XP.
- False rejects of what the lesson teaches: *ho preso*, *ho bevuto*, *sono andato* ("not in the dictionary yet"); *il martedì*, *la notte*, *di notte* in the time slot whose own options are *il lunedì*, *la sera*; *ho caldo*, *sto bene* in "Bene grazie, ma ___".
- The slot's category only sorts suggestions, never filters; the English line keeps "____" after a free entry; three different sizes are shown for one download.

On-device AI
- The fit scorer, run with the real model, marks the lesson's own bank words *libero* and *il gelato* "odd here" while ungrammatical *non* and *mai* are merely "unusual": the scale is anchored to the worst authored option and the options passed include the deliberately wrong distractors. It is not a sound learner-facing signal.
- The assistant never runs: `afterTurn` requires two or more generic reactions on a turn; all 62 learner turns have exactly one. `releaseAssistant` is never called, so once loaded the engine stays resident. The crash-loop breaker and guards are well engineered.
- Useful words: 78/78 notes correct; the deck is absent from the default Learn view (it lives in the alternate Sezioni layout and under Words › Decks); no lesson sequence; no links from the course or workshop.

### 4.5 Dictionary, verb engine, reference and games (`data-and-reference.md`)

- **Clean data.** 200 words and 80 verbs read in full: 0 errors, 7 doubtful. Whole set: no empty fields, no Italian in glosses, all -co/-go/-cia/-gia/-io/neuter/invariable plurals correct, 995 adjective form sets correct, 0 disagreements between the data's `irregular` flag and the engine.
- **High: shadowed senses.** `build-data.mjs` keeps the lowest-level record per headword; C2 files were authored with rare senses of common words and no lower file has the common sense, so *macchia* is only "Mediterranean scrub", *trasporto* only "rapture", *mora* only "default (late payment)", *credenza* "sideboard", *giostra* "joust", plus about fifteen more. Ordinary words sit at C2 (*borgo*, *ventre*, *crepuscolo*, *rione*, *prassi*).
- **High: accent rules never fire.** `referenceEntry.js:382,387,468,473,486` test `/[àèéìòù]$/` and `/(tà|tù)$/` on a `fold()`ed string that has no accents, so *caffè*, *città*, *università*, *virtù* (178 nouns) get a generic or wrong plural and gender explanation.
- Medium: `expr` holds ~320 plain noun phrases that lose article, plural and gender and are excluded from the article and plural games; one exact duplicate (*tasso di interesse* / *tasso d'interesse*) and eight near-duplicate idioms across levels; all 55 `aux: both` verbs are presented avere-first in tables and drilled "(use avere)", including essere-dominant *crescere*, *guarire*, *mancare*, *aumentare*; cloze distractors are frequently also correct (*perciò / quindi / dunque*) or give the answer away by inflection; `grammar.json` covers 12 topics while the course teaches about 15 more (possessives, relatives, subjunctive, conditionals, passive/si, reported speech, connectives); the sentence builder accepts the source word order only; 14 common verbs are missing (*parere*, *sedere*, *udire*, *stringere*, *rifare*…); the "why irregular" panel labels accepted alternatives as "regular would be" and derives *bere* → "bo · bi · be".
- Engine edge cases: *esserci* compounds built with avere; bare participle for *farcela/avercela*; no negative imperative; impersonal verbs shown in six persons.

### 4.6 Architecture and code organisation (`architecture.md`)

Measured: 615 bytes per event; 10 ms per answer at 250 events, 124 ms at 3,000, 0.4 s merge on sync.
- **High: boot path.** `store.js` → `integration.js` → `lesson-content.js` → `progressive-content.js` → the 640 KB generated verb table: the boot closure is 25 modules / 1,127 KB (487 KB without the table).
- **High: double dispatch.** `learn/verb/:id` loads `learnVerb.js` (with `walkthrough.js` and the games engine, 348 KB), which imports `learnAdaptive.js`, which imports `learnJourney.js`; the `legacy=1` branch is produced only by a test.
- **High: duplication.** Five lesson players; six multiple-choice renderers; an accent bar in six places under three class names; the keyboard-fit block pasted into five views; `components.js` re-implements the conjugator's regular paradigm; HTML escaping ×5, the Italian normaliser ×15, person labels ×4; four lookup lexica with four id prefixes; one session map holding six session shapes with nine version counters and a key-aliasing workaround for the workshop.
- High: 56 game selectors defined in both `app.css` §14 and `games.css`; 72 identical declaration blocks; 62 `!important`; `grammar-course.css` is minified; DESIGN.md §7.1 still names three feature sheets (there are thirteen).
- High: the retained generations (original walkthrough, v1 adaptive loop, the 129-lesson earlier course, the pre-v2 progressive flow) cost about 890 KB of the 10 MB precache, three browser suites, three node checks and branches in seven files, behind a toggle that is on by default.
- Medium: 29 % of JavaScript bytes sit on lines longer than 160 characters (longest 2,083) with no formatter; the router's own `route`/`navigate` are unused (16 files set `location.hash`); 91 exports are never imported (11 dead functions); eight authoring one-offs (550 KB) sit among 42 CI scripts in `tools/`; `docs/` is 2.4 MB of which 2.1 MB is JSON audit output with seven orphan documents and no index; eight of 37 node checks take 86 % of the 198 s local run; every browser job reinstalls Playwright (about 12 runner-minutes per run).
- All seven findings of the 29 September audit are resolved or superseded; of the 1 October observations, README, deploy gating and the manual service-worker bump are resolved, the JSON output is out of the site but still in the repo, the precache grew to 134 files / 10 MB, and no physical-device check is recorded.

### 4.7 Performance, offline and accessibility (`performance.md`)

Measured in Chromium with phone emulation on a loaded host, so absolute times are pessimistic and relative ones hold.

- **Critical: the first screen waits for everything.** Boot awaits the dictionary, all thirteen course packs and `attachLessonVocabulary()` before rendering: 64 requests, 8.4 MB raw (1.9 MB gzip), 1.23 MB of JavaScript. First screen 1.7 s on a fast network at 1× CPU, 3.3 s at 4×, 11 s on slow 4G with gzip, 48 s uncompressed; served from the service-worker cache still 2.6 s (1×) and about 9 s (4×) because roughly a second of main-thread JSON parse and module evaluation gates paint. `attachLessonVocabulary()` alone costs 580 ms (1×) to 1.2 s (4×) at boot although `grammarLesson()` already attaches lazily.
- **Critical: evidence-log scaling, measured in the browser.** One answer costs 36–50 ms median at 2,000 events, 0.5 s at 6,000, 2.6 s at 24,000; Home's `reviewItems()` + `recommendLesson()` 2.4 s, 9 s and 12 s; a reboot with 24,000 events takes 18 s and the profile is 14 MB. The localStorage crash-safety mirror silently stops working once the profile passes about 2.5 MB (around 4,000 events).
- **High: service-worker update cost.** An update with nothing but `sw.js` changed still issues 133 conditional requests (4.9 s fast, 9–10 s on slow 4G); the first install fetches 37–65 full bodies the page never used. The precache is 10.36 MB raw (2.29 MB gzip), 12.2 MB in Cache Storage. Fix: per-file hashes from `stamp-sw.mjs`, copy unchanged entries from the previous cache.
- **High: a truncated course pack kills Learn, Course, placement and every lesson route** with a raw JSON error, because `loadGrammarCourse()` rethrows for all views; Home survives.
- **Offline and update are genuinely robust (verified):** 21/21 routes work offline with zero failed requests (lessons, workshop, review, games, reference); an update while a lesson is open reloads into the new build with events, session and XP intact; IndexedDB blocked or aborting, localStorage quota, a v1 profile, a 500 on the dictionary and schema skew all degrade correctly.
- **Runtime is clean:** at most five blur panes per screen (budget six), only the three orbs and the ticker animate, zero idle timers, reduced motion stops every animation, no detached DOM after 20 navigation loops.
- **Accessibility:** every control is keyboard-reachable with a visible focus ring, no unnamed icon buttons, no role-less custom controls; but tap-to-reveal spans are buttons with hidden content and no `aria-expanded`, there is no live region before feedback is injected, headings are missing on the verb lesson, games and Me, the Italian greeting, date, word of the day, section titles and choice buttons lack `lang="it"`, 10–11 px mono labels are everywhere (8 px on the dial), `maximum-scale=1` blocks zoom in a home-screen app, and 1.3× text breaks the Home cards and posters. ## 5. Reorganisation proposals

### 5.1 The product: one path, one plan, one level

| Tab | Should hold | Should lose |

| **Home** | Greeting, streak and XP; **Today's plan** as an ordered list of three or four steps (resume, review *n* due, next lesson, workshop lesson) with one Continue that opens step 1; word and verb of the day | The level ribbon, the games reel, the decorative counters |
| **Learn** | One view: stage header (stage · unit · n/N · Change); the path as units of lesson rows with the next one highlighted; workshop lessons interleaved where their grammar is met; verb chapters at the unit that teaches the tense; the unit's vocabulary block and Parole utili; an "In progress" strip only when non-empty | The Panoramica/Sezioni split, the dial, the Laboratorio reel, the Review pane |
| **Play** | **Review first** (*n* due · Start review → a mixed 10–20 question runner; Practise ahead), then the game reels and the source picker | Nothing |
| **Words** | Search; Recently viewed; lists, bank and custom words; Parole utili; browse by level and topic; grammar topics; **one** detail page (the codex, with the entry's action row) | The Reference index, the entry/codex split |
| **Me** | Identity; progress (stage, words, verbs, workshop, streak, heat map, queue); goals (level = stage, daily targets); a **Settings** row opening a sheet (display and sound, lessons, backup, sync, users, reset) | The 10,000 px scroll |

Rules: `learnModel()` is the single source for Home, Learn and Me; the course stage is the one level and the scope page becomes "Study from…" filters inside Words and Play; one lesson shell and one completion screen (stamp, +XP, learned items, Next and Back, one close, one options menu, a stable title) shared by course, verb, word and workshop players; English is the UI language, Italian is for learnable content, stamps and a fixed set of named places shown "Italian · English" on first appearance (*Officina delle frasi · Sentence workshop*, *Parole utili · Useful words*, *Le parole di oggi · Today's words*, *Le mie frasi · My sentences*); UK spelling throughout.

### 5.2 Progression: one mastery ladder, one scheduler, one plan

| State | Word | Verb target | Course target |
|---|---|---|---|
| introduced | any lesson, board or drill completed (today's tick) | chapter teaching seen | lesson finished |
| recognised | two first-attempt recognition hits in different sessions | n/a | guided correct |
| recalled | one unaided typed recall in a later session ≥ 8 h after introduction | current *ready* | current *ready* |
| remembered | a second unaided recall ≥ 1 day later | current *remembered* | current *remembered* |

- "Learned" in the UI means **recalled**; the learned list, level rings, scope filter and game sources read that state. The end of a lesson says "introduced · comes back tomorrow".
- All word paths (lesson, board, workshop drill, game) write events on the lesson objective ids; views never call `markLearned`. The board keeps its policy and counts as introduced plus one recognition hit. Workshop drills count first-attempt hits only.
- The first review of a word is one screen: recall choice if only introduced, otherwise typed recall with a letter bank on the first failure; that single typed success promotes to recalled and pays the 10 XP.
- One schedule per word entry (quality 4 typed, 3 recognition, 1 miss; recognition never lowers ease); verb review serves every due target of a case in one session; course review items are the lesson, not the facet; any correct answer anywhere that maps onto a due objective advances it; `store.items` scheduling is retired and `dueEntries(store)` feeds Home, Learn and the games' "Due" source.
- `plan(store, now)` returns, in order: due reviews (capped per entry, interleaved verb/word/course), the next course lesson, one verb case from that lesson's `related` links, new words only while overdue entries < `dailyNew`, and the workshop lesson whose grammar matches. Home Continue walks the plan; the Together session *is* the plan.
- XP per answer: 0 supported, 1 recognition, 3 unaided production, +5 on promotion to recalled, +10 on remembered; completion bonuses move to the promotion events. Level = course stage, with "A1 complete" = all A1 lessons complete and the stage's anchor verb cases remembered; the rings show course progress per stage with vocabulary as a secondary count. Streak = days with at least one recorded answer.
- Verb chapters: cap at about 12 checks per sitting, schedule the rest into review, weight repetition toward irregular cells and missed cells, allow a shorter path for a regular verb once the pattern is proven on another, and count the verb learned after Present plus one past tense.
- Migration: `LEARNING_VERSION 6` whose analysis computes the new states; existing `item.learned` without a recalled event becomes introduced (say so once; keep XP already earned); game and workshop events are re-keyed to lesson objective ids in normalisation.

### 5.3 The course: a real syllabus for A1–A2, authored by hand

Spine (full unit lists in `course-content.md` §6):
- **A1, 12 units, about 45 lessons**: who you are (essere all persons, tu/Lei, nationalities, question words); things around you (articles, plurals, questo/quello, colours, c'è/ci sono); having and counting (avere, numbers 0–100, age, prices); family and people (suo/sua, nostro/vostro, family nouns, descriptive adjectives, professions); everyday actions (regular present, days, stare/fare/andare in full); food and the café (-ere/-ire/-isc, modals, ordering); going places (andare/venire/uscire, prepositions and articulated forms, directions); likes and time (piacere with all indirect pronouns, the clock, months, dates, weather); routine (reflexives, prima/poi, mentre); requests (tu/Lei imperatives, phone, shop, doctor, body); yesterday (passato prossimo, ten irregular participles, ieri/fa/scorso); checkpoint. Pronunciation: fold the nine lessons into four with recorded minimal pairs.
- **A2, 13 units**: past events; background and story (imperfetto all persons); in progress; direct objects; recipients; plans (future all persons); politeness and wishes (conditional full paradigm); comparing (done correctly, with tanto…quanto and migliore); instructions (voi/Lei, negatives, pronoun + imperative, va'/fa'/di'/sta'); place and time; describing and shopping; linking; checkpoint.
- **B1/B2**: keep the frames; merge over-split form lessons; replace the *ce ne* lessons with a *ci/ce/ne* lesson; add modals in the past, stare per, volerci, bisogna; give the full subjunctive and conditional paradigms before use; readings 80–300 words and listenings 30–60 s with two voices.
- **C1/C2**: keep themes and inputs; replace the graded B-level grammar with C-level discriminations in Italian (register, collocation, cohesion editing, reformulation with accepted variants); item-specific explanations; different passages per lesson.

Pipeline rules: every model and item hand-written, generators become validators; a lexical budget per unit (20–30 new dictionary words) enforced by a check, targeting ≥ 600 word types by the end of A1 and ≥ 1,200 by the end of A2; variety gates (no prompt > 25 % of a level, no context reused across lessons, hint ≠ explanation, hint never contains the answer); a paradigm-completeness gate (a form may be asked only if a teach step in the lesson or its prerequisite chain lists it); distractors drawn from real dictionary and conjugator forms; `accepted` variants by default and `strict` only where the contrast is the target; input floors per level; pronunciation items reference a recorded clip; native review as a hard gate; facets as real contrasts; minutes computed from steps.

### 5.4 The workshop and on-device AI

Deterministic first (the authoritative layer): grade a typed form that differs from the slot's required form of the same lemma as *incorrect* with the agreement or person explanation (keep silent re-inflection for the English path only); accept auxiliary + participle and multiword time phrases; unwrap *ho {}* and *sto {}* as well as *sono {}*; let the slot category filter, not just sort; exclude negation and polarity adverbs from adverb slots and quantifiers from adjective slots; block *più/meno + migliore/peggiore*; add a predicate-noun gender check to Say it yourself; fill the English blank after a free entry; one download size figure; show the speaker-gender control inside the player; let reactions carry their own next turn or make them statements.

Then the models, under one rule: the LLM *selects* (constrained decoding over indices) or writes English; any Italian it writes is shown only after the deterministic verifier (dictionary forms, conjugator, agreement, an absolute fit floor), otherwise an authored fallback. Ranked by value and risk: (1) intent → build, the learner types an English intent and the model returns role indices the engine composes; (2) a meaning verdict for Say it yourself (fine / odd / nonsense with an authored reason) that never blocks Keep; (3) "explain my mistake" in English in any player, with an Italian-quote filter; (4) reactive replies by selection from a six-to-ten reaction pool (which also fixes the dead trigger), later slot templates filled by the engine; (5) fresh verified example sentences; (6) free generated replies, (7) a bounded tutor chat and (8) portfolio writing feedback only with a 4B-class model after measurement. Pronunciation is out of WebLLM's scope (a separate ASR track).

Models: Qwen3-1.7B q4f16 as the default (Apache 2.0, prebuilt in WebLLM 0.2.85, about 850 MB, 2.0 GB VRAM at 4k context), 0.6B as the selection-only fallback, 4B gated on a memory measurement; avoid Gemma (not prebuilt, restrictive licence) and Phi (weak Italian). Retire the learner-facing fit note now; keep the scorer files only if repurposed as an internal ranker or verifier, otherwise remove the 83 MB. Add a "remove model" control, prune old WebLLM caches on a model change, and call `releaseAssistant` on lesson exit. Phase 1 is a day on the phone: 0.6B/1.7B/4B at 1k context, prefill and decode tokens per second, 30-minute memory survival, battery per 20 picks, cold-cache load; pass = 20 picks, 0 trips, median ≤ 2.5 s, p95 ≤ 5 s.

## 8. Verified versus inferred

Verified in this audit: every lesson id, quotation, count, file:line reference and reproduction cited in §4 (read in the packs or source, or produced by a script, simulation or browser run in the session; the most serious claims in each area were reproduced independently a second time); the live deploy state; the dictionary sample results; the conjugation results; the simulations' numbers.

Inferred, and marked as such in the area reports: estimated minutes per lesson (from per-screen timings, not people); CEFR placement judgements; naturalness judgements by native-level reviewers without a corpus check; phone latencies and memory for the assistant models (no public Safari WebGPU benchmarks; measure first); runner-minute estimates; real-device boot times (the performance numbers are Chromium on a loaded four-core host, so they overstate absolute times while the ratios hold); WebKit storage-eviction behaviour; that A2–C2 findings generalise from the sampled lessons.

Not done: a physical iPhone run (no device in the session); the owner's real profile was not inspected; no live user data of any kind was read.
