# A1–A2 grammar course: coverage and sources

The two beginner packs contain **48 lessons** in **11 units**. Each lesson has one focused objective, two short teaching cards, at least four independent practice variants, and a six-minute estimate. Across the packs there are 193 authored questions using choice, match, order, and brief typed answers. The prerequisites form a sequence within each level; A2 begins after `a1-first-past`. The course links to existing dictionary entries, including the corresponding verb learning case (`present`, `past`, `background`, `future`, or `condizionale`).

## A1 coverage

| Unit | Lesson IDs | Main work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `a1-first-sentences` | `a1-sounds-spelling`, `a1-first-sentence`, `a1-essere`, `a1-avere` | Read c/g/ch/gh/sc cues; build an ordinary sentence; identify people and origin; express possession, age, and physical states. |
| `a1-nouns-agreement` | `a1-noun-gender`, `a1-indefinite-articles`, `a1-definite-articles`, `a1-plurals`, `a1-adjective-agreement` | Use gender clues and lexical exceptions; choose un/uno/una/un’ and il/lo/la/i/gli/le; make regular and spelling-preserving plurals; agree adjectives. |
| `a1-present` | `a1-present-are`, `a1-present-ere`, `a1-present-ire`, `a1-common-irregular-present`, `a1-questions-negation`, `a1-c-e-ci-sono` | Conjugate the three present families, including -isc-; use fare and andare; ask and negate; distinguish singular and plural existential phrases. |
| `a1-describe-locate` | `a1-possessives`, `a1-demonstratives`, `a1-simple-prepositions`, `a1-articulated-prepositions`, `a1-quantity-time` | Express whose, this/that, city/country/origin/company, fused preposition–article forms, quantity agreement, and clock time. |
| `a1-daily-interaction` | `a1-modal-requests`, `a1-tu-commands`, `a1-piacere`, `a1-first-past` | Ask with modal + infinitive, give a familiar instruction, state likes with singular/plural agreement, and report a first completed event with avere. |

A1 intentionally introduces a useful `ho mangiato` pattern before the fuller A2 treatment. Noun endings are presented as clues rather than guarantees; *pane* and *mano* make that limitation concrete. The spelling lesson is a reading and writing aid and requires no audio-only exercise.

## A2 coverage

| Unit | Lesson IDs | Main work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `a2-completed-past` | `a2-passato-regular`, `a2-passato-essere`, `a2-irregular-participles`, `a2-choose-auxiliary` | Build the compound past with regular and frequent irregular participles; agree participles with *essere*; choose an auxiliary from the verb’s construction rather than movement alone. |
| `a2-past-aspect` | `a2-imperfetto`, `a2-past-contrast`, `a2-progressive-now`, `a2-progressive-past` | Describe habits and background, distinguish completed events, and focus on an action underway now or at a past moment. Progressive cards explicitly keep ordinary habits and stable possession in a simple tense. |
| `a2-pronouns-reflexives` | `a2-reflexive-present`, `a2-reflexive-past`, `a2-direct-object-pronouns`, `a2-indirect-object-pronouns`, `a2-pronoun-placement` | Use everyday reflexive routines in present and past; distinguish *lo/la/li/le* from recipient *gli/le*; place an object pronoun before a modal or after its infinitive. |
| `a2-future-conditional` | `a2-future-regular`, `a2-future-irregular`, `a2-conditional-politeness` | Make future plans with regular endings and frequent altered stems; make polite requests with *vorrei* and *potrei*. |
| `a2-comparison-amount` | `a2-comparatives`, `a2-superlatives`, `a2-quantity-patterns` | Compare two referents or two qualities with *di/che*; distinguish relative and absolute superlatives; use *qualche* with singular nouns and agree approximate amounts. |
| `a2-directions-links` | `a2-polite-commands`, `a2-negative-commands`, `a2-da-place-time`, `a2-linking-ideas`, `a2-relative-che` | Give courteous *Lei* and negative *tu/voi* instructions; use *da* for origin, a person’s place, and continuing duration; connect cause/sequence; add a defining relative clause with *che*. |

The present and past progressive lessons emphasize viewpoint and their restrictions rather than translating every English “-ing” mechanically. The formal *Lei* command is taught as a practical form; a systematic explanation of the subjunctive belongs to later levels. Direct-object pronouns are practiced with simple finite verbs; compound-past participle agreement after a preceding object pronoun can be treated in a later lesson.

## Source notes

The [Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4) informed the scope of concrete A1 exchanges and connected A2 everyday communication. CEFR descriptors describe communicative performance, not a mandatory grammar syllabus; the topic sequence here is an editorial design for Parola.

Treccani's [article reference](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/articolo_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/), [verb conjugation reference](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/coniugazione-verbale_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/), and [preposition reference](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/preposizione/) support the A1 forms and agreement patterns. Its [passato prossimo](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/indicativo-passato-prossimo_%28La-grammatica-italiana%29/) and [imperfetto](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/indicativo-imperfetto_%28La-grammatica-italiana%29/) entries support A2’s event/background distinction and auxiliary choices. [Periphrastic structures](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/strutture-perifrastiche_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/) support the progressive limits; [personal pronouns](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pronomi-personali_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/) support object and recipient forms. [Comparison](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/comparazione/) supports *di/che* and superlatives; [negative imperative](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/imperativo-negativo_%28La-grammatica-italiana%29/) supports *non* + infinitive and pronoun placement; [relative clauses](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/proposizioni-relative_%28La-grammatica-italiana%29/) support defining *che*.

These are original short explanations and examples, not copied source text. Every question includes feedback and a hint tailored to its specific sentence. Type tasks ask only for a word or brief phrase; defensible alternate placements or apostrophe spellings are accepted when relevant.
